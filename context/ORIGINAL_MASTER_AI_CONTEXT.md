# Familiar — Master AI Context Pack

**Competition:** TOKYO GLOBAL INNOVATION CHALLENGE 2026 (TGIC)  
**Version:** 1.0, reconstructed and substantially revised on 13 October 2026  
**Language:** English, for competition preparation and continuity  
**Product:** Secondhand clothing marketplace with optional personal-reference discovery and guided condition evidence  
**Status:** Chosen concept and researched future-product specification. A production marketplace, trained model, measured accuracy, customer traction and signed partnerships are not established by the supplied record.

> Familiar turns an ordinary secondhand clothing search into a better-informed choice. Anyone can browse by style, size and price. A buyer can optionally choose a garment they already enjoy wearing to find and compare items with similar observed behaviour. Sellers follow guided camera tasks; buyers inspect original evidence of visible condition, supported comparisons and clearly stated unknowns.

**Working public line:** Search normally. Match personally. Inspect the evidence.  
**Emotional brand line:** Find clothes that feel familiar.  
**Precise explanation beside it:** Compare captured garment behaviour with something you already know; physical touch and hidden properties are not guaranteed.

## Conclusions

1. **The strongest idea is one coherent decision system.** Ordinary discovery, condition evidence and optional comparison answer different parts of the same purchase question: what is this item, and is it likely to suit what I know and want? [M01–M03, M07–M09]
2. **Ambition belongs in the experience; evidence belongs in the claims.** Show the complete future journey while stating exactly which media, analyses and transactions are simulated. [M03, M05–M08]
3. **TGIC preparation should focus on affected people, solution fit and a visible implementation path.** A polished demo supports communication but cannot supply missing firsthand accounts or traction. [M04–M07]
4. **The personal-reference mechanism is a valuable hypothesis.** Its decisive test compares it with equally good evidence without a reference, measuring calibrated expectations rather than only confidence or conversion. [M07–M08]
5. **Marketplace economics need improvement before a viability claim.** Unsold capture cost, support, processing, returns and acquisition are material; base-case scale alone does not solve thin contribution. [M10–M11]
6. **Useful reuse is the impact objective.** Kept and worn garments, fair seller outcomes and measured displacement are more credible than a carbon-saving number on every purchase. [M12]

### 1.3 The product's three cooperating layers

**Familiar Marketplace:** ordinary clothing discovery, measurements, prices, seller communication, favourites, checkout, delivery and resolution. Selection, affordability and reliable fulfilment remain essential. A shopper uninterested in wardrobe capture should still understand the product and complete a purchase.

**Familiar Match:** a reusable private reference helps the buyer find and interpret alternatives. The user selects what they like or dislike about that reference. The service retains ordinary filters, searches compatible available listings, shows ranked candidates, and offers per-property comparisons. An owned item is not automatically a liked item. Matching drape does not establish fit, cleanliness or overall preference.

**Familiar Evidence:** original views, task coverage, condition locations, seller declarations, reviewed suggestions, disputed findings and unknowns. These records support trust without pretending to physically inspect an unrecorded region. A profile may have excellent coverage and poor condition; condition and evidence completeness are separate.

### 1.4 Evidence atlas and useful assistant questions

Every claim has a type, source and review state. A region marker is an entry into an evidence atlas: full frame, close-up, timestamp, garment location, capture task and statement it supports. The buyer can move from “pilling at cuff” to the original footage and back to the purchase decision. A cropped image alone should not hide surrounding context.

| Buyer question | Intended grounded response |
|---|---|
| “Find a black blouse under Rp150,000.” | Parse visible filters and show ordinary available results. No private reference required. |
| “Something that moves like my blue blouse.” | Ask which reference and properties matter; retrieve compatible items and disclose unsupported dimensions. |
| “Is this softer?” | Explain that skin softness is not established by these captures; show supported surface/movement differences instead. |
| “Where is the wear?” | Open condition locations and source evidence, with seller/review status. |
| “Is it clean and authentic?” | Keep those properties unverified by camera evidence; distinguish any seller declaration or separate service. |
| “Why isn't this a match?” | Explain an actual difference or insufficient evidence, rather than inventing a negative judgment. |

The assistant is a navigation and evidence interpreter. It must not turn persuasive language into an unsupported sensory promise. Useful future extensions include saved comparison intentions, demand requests, repair referrals and permissioned platform integration; none should displace the three central layers in the competition narrative.

### Conclusions and business design

#### Market thesis: reuse with clearer expectations

Familiar's core business proposition is: **Search normally. Match personally. Buy with better evidence.** People may browse and buy without uploading a wardrobe. Sellers create ordinary listings, then follow guided phone captures that make visible wear and observable fabric behavior easier to inspect. Buyers can optionally choose a garment they already like and compare candidate listings against that personal reference. The marketplace must still compete on selection, price, size, delivery and dispute resolution; the additional layers are useful only if they improve an actual purchase decision.

The social problem is a possible mismatch between existing wearable supply and confident reuse demand. UNEP's waste framing makes a circular-clothing intervention relevant, but does not show that this product solves that problem. Japan's present material-flow and policy pages demonstrate a measurable domestic discard issue and an explicit policy ambition. Indonesia's internet and broad commerce sources indicate access to digital transactions, while leaving adoption gaps, geography and affordability important. None establishes the frequency of fabric uncertainty among Familiar's target buyers. That specific problem remains a research question to test. [MB01–MB07,H01]

A defensible pitch therefore moves from macro problem to a bounded mechanism: wearable garments already held domestically can remain unused because listing evidence and buyer confidence are incomplete; Familiar intends to make that evidence easier to create and interpret. Successful matching might increase useful wear and reduce expectation mismatch. It might also merely shift existing sales between platforms or stimulate extra consumption. Those alternatives belong in the impact design.

For Indonesia, state the intended supply policy precisely: **domestically sourced, lawfully owned secondhand garments; no imported used-clothing bales or cross-border used-clothing import service.** Imported while new and subsequently worn locally is not the same factual situation as imported while used; exact eligibility, exceptions and documentation require local review. A seller's assertion is not proof of lawful origin. Familiar should record sourcing declarations, request merchant procurement evidence when warranted, restrict suspicious bulk suppliers and operate a takedown/escalation process. Camera evidence cannot certify origin, hygiene, chemical fiber composition, odor or absence of hidden defects. Domestic resale is a plausible compliant route, not a blanket legal clearance for every listing. [MB08–MB10]

Japan is an optional, separate domestic expansion hypothesis. The domestic CtoC context and circular-fashion target support relevance, while incumbent density, Japanese service expectations, language, payment and legal obligations add difficulty. A Tokyo pilot could recruit local wardrobe sellers and licensed reuse merchants without any Indonesia–Japan used-clothing trade. If Familiar buys inventory, consigns garments or acts as a dealer, its Japanese obligations may differ from those of a listing intermediary. Tokyo Police specifically describes counterparty and website disclosures for dealers. Entity role, platform structure, consumer rules and permit requirements must be resolved before transactions. TGIC participation does not make Japan the approved first market. [MB04,MB07,MB12]

#### Liquidity and geographic sequencing

A secondhand garment is usually a single-unit listing. After purchase, it disappears; catalog size alone cannot establish useful availability. Liquidity exists at the intersection of category, dimensions/size, price, style, location/shipping and freshness. A marketplace with many irrelevant garments can still leave a buyer with no acceptable options. Wardrobe matching makes this more demanding because compatible candidates also need comparable evidence.

Commercial rollout should concentrate supply and demand within one recruited community and shipping region before claiming nationwide service. The founder's strongest real access may favor Malang, Jakarta/Depok or another city; this chapter does not choose a city without team evidence. Candidate initial categories are tops/blouses and lightweight knitwear where drape/stretch information can matter. This is a hypothesis to be tested against local garment use, pricing and climate. Dresses, heavier garments and more complex categories can expand only when capture and dispute burden are understood.

Recruit a mixture of repeat domestic merchants and household sellers. Merchants provide recurring inventory; household sellers preserve the mission and supply diversity. Avoid relying on imported-bale merchants merely because they offer large volume. Use assisted capture sessions, seller bundles and demand-led requests to fill weak size/price cells. Merchant concentration and seller source eligibility should be monitored alongside total listing count.

Suggested pilot monitoring includes qualified active listings per important size/price cell; search sessions with at least three credible candidates; no-result rate; median time to first relevant result; capture completion; seller response time; 30-day sell-through; fulfillment rate; complaint reason; and repeat satisfied purchase. Numerical thresholds should be approved after baseline data. These are proposed decision metrics, not previously achieved results. A polished full-product demonstration can show every intended feature now; staged commercial rollout does not dictate its scope.

#### Bottom-up market sizing

No reliable clothing-only domestic Indonesian resale GMV was verified here. BPS commerce/internet and METI all-category CtoC figures should not be used as the numerator of a clothing TAM, nor should garment-waste tonnes be priced into revenue. For an application, present a reproducible estimation method with scenario counts visibly labeled. [MB05–MB07,MB19]

Define annual demand GMV as **eligible active buyers × orders per buyer × mean garment price**. Define serviceable supply GMV independently as **active sellers × eligible listings per seller × sell-through × mean price**. Addressable completed volume is constrained by both sides; demand cannot exceed fulfillable relevant supply. Platform commission revenue is completed net merchandise GMV × effective take rate, after discounts/refunds under the chosen accounting policy. GMV is not company revenue.

Supply subsidies, capture events and merchant onboarding all consume cash even when buyer acquisition looks cheap.

Sensitivity is commercially material: at the base assumptions, one extra commission percentage point adds IDR 2,000/order; one extra processing percentage point on IDR 225,000 removes IDR 2,250; a one percentage point increase in unrecovered loss removes IDR 2,000; doubling return subsidies removes IDR 1,000. Buyer-visible affordability is the total checkout: base IDR 200,000 plus 25,000 shipping before applicable taxes. Familiar's own VAT, income tax, invoicing and appointment obligations require entity-specific advice. Do not blindly add 0.5% to every order. Refunds can reverse commission while leave processing, support, shipment or inference costs behind; build attempt-level and completed-order reconciliations. Logistics insurance limits, COD rejection, chargebacks, transit damage, hygiene complaints and return-window design need actual contracts. [MB11]

#### Impact theory and measurement

The intended chain is: guided capture and interpretable comparisons → better expectations and useful matching → satisfactory transfers of existing wearable garments → more real wear or longer use → possible reduction in discard and new-production demand. Each arrow is a testable hypothesis. Increased listings, badges or gross transactions are outputs; additional wear, affordability, seller net income and avoided production are outcomes.

| Outcome | Proposed measurement | Counterfactual or caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Better purchase expectations | Post-delivery expectation survey, complaint reason, repeat wear, condition mismatch rate. | Compare with good conventional listings and video; no assumed return reduction. |
| Additional useful reuse | At 30/90 days, ask whether worn and approximate wear frequency; identify repeat transfers without double counting. | Seller might already have sold/donated through another channel. |
| Affordability | All-in cost, value satisfaction and usable wears; stratify by price and shipping burden. | Cheap unused garments are poor value; shipping can erase savings. |
| Seller economic benefit | Net proceeds minus capture labor, fees, packaging and variable costs; repeat seller participation. | Higher platform GMV does not equal higher household income. |
| Environmental additionality | Garment type/mass, likely alternative purchase, pre-sale destination, actual use, shipping/returns/cleaning. | Rebound, substitution to other secondhand platforms and exported reuse change results. |

Count a transaction as a transfer, not automatically a garment “saved from landfill.” Ask what the seller would otherwise have done: retain, wear, donate, resell elsewhere, recycle or discard. Ask what the buyer would otherwise have bought: new, secondhand elsewhere, nothing, or a different item. Distinguish intention from subsequent behavior and report uncertainty. The strongest evidence would combine randomized or appropriately controlled listing comparisons with longitudinal wear/return data; this protocol is proposed, not executed.

Do not assign a universal CO2 saving per garment or use a national average manufacturing footprint as certain transaction-level avoided emissions. A transparent scenario could estimate **completed transfers × additional-use probability × new-purchase displacement probability × relevant avoided-production burden − additional processing, transport, returns and care burden**, with compatible boundaries and uncertainty. Probabilities can be dependent, so collect joint outcomes rather than mechanically multiplying unsupported percentages. Rebound includes extra cheap purchases and use of resale proceeds for new purchases. Benefits can be smaller, zero or negative for some transactions.

Familiar can align its intended activities with SDG 12 through reuse and SDG 8 through possible microbusiness income, but should not imply certification or proven target contribution. Domestic reuse reduces reliance on exporting an unresolved waste problem; MOE explicitly notes that export-based reuse can affect receiving-country industries. Source compliance, privacy, fair fees, accessible explanations and repair/referral pathways are substantive operating responsibilities. [MB02–MB04,H01]

### Conclusions

#### Touch is a real information problem with more than one consumer function

Familiar’s defensible starting proposition is that garment decisions may require information that ordinary photographs communicate poorly. The material-versus-geometric distinction in CLF02 explains why adding a better silhouette image may leave uncertainty about a T-shirt unresolved. Yet the evidence also permits substitution: understandable descriptions of relevant properties can help remote evaluation. It does not justify announcing that a camera reproduces touching, that every consumer needs the same information, or that digitally confident buyers will like the garment after delivery [CLF01–CLF03].

Instrumental and autotelic touch suggest different mechanisms. Someone seeking lower cling or a certain drape may appreciate a comparison. Someone enjoying physical rummaging may still prefer an offline encounter. Treat NFT as a possible moderator to measure, rather than an acquisition segment whose demand has already been established. The null moderation in CLF03 is useful counterevidence. It could reflect the product, manipulation, measurement, or population; it is neither proof that individual differences never matter nor grounds to assume that they always predict adoption.

#### Personal reference is a plausible innovation, with a specific burden of proof

The proposed owned garment supplies a user-specific point of comparison: “this listing falls more loosely than the reference” can be easier to interpret than an isolated technical score. This is an inference from information-substitution and bounded knowledge-transfer evidence, not an effect demonstrated by the reviewed literature [CLF02–CLF05]. The reviewed studies do not establish that a personal reference beats an unfamiliar reference, a clear attribute explanation, or standardized video alone.

The reference must also encode what the person likes. Ownership cannot establish preference: a garment may be kept for work, disliked, altered, worn out, or comfortable only in a particular context. Ask the buyer to identify relevant attributes and permit dislikes. Similar appearance or fabric behaviour should not collapse into predicted body fit, softness, thermal comfort, fibre identity, or overall satisfaction. Attribute-specific statements preserve both matches and differences. CLF05 makes the caution substantive: familiar framing can guide expectations, so an appealing comparison could create mistaken expectations as well as useful understanding.

The strongest commercial alternative is simpler evidence. CLF03’s null zoom result argues for comparing meaningful information treatments, not equating interface richness with value. CLF04 makes demonstration video promising, but its measured imagery and intentions leave perceptual correctness unresolved. Familiar’s differentiation will require evidence that the reference changes the *right* decisions, not merely the number of positive judgments.

#### Condition evidence addresses part of resale trust

Secondhand clothing brings several uncertainties together: what the garment is like, whether its condition is represented faithfully, whether its attributes suit the buyer, and whether the transaction will be handled reliably. CL14 and CL15 provide conceptual vocabulary for product versus seller uncertainty and quality versus preference fit; their body-level results were not verified here. Keep these constructs distinct in Familiar’s design and research. A reliable seller can sell an accurately represented garment that the buyer dislikes.

CLF07–CLF08 show why one reassurance feature cannot resolve the entire resale decision. Condition evidence may address some quality concerns, while ordinary search addresses effort and assortment. Identity, social acceptance, price, cleanliness, style, and occasion can still determine rejection. CLF06 supports retaining discovery and enjoyable browsing. A compulsory wardrobe-capture step would impose a cost before these shoppers can encounter the marketplace’s ordinary value; keeping it optional is a design inference to test, not a validated adoption result.

Guided capture should request useful coverage, display the actual item, preserve defect close-ups, and distinguish seller declarations from observed evidence. CLF09 supports investigating item-specific imagery, but its trust advantage should not become a claim that photographs certify true condition. “Evidence captured” and “visible issue flagged” describe processes more precisely than “high quality verified.” A visible-condition badge needs a defined scope, missing-area disclosure, seller correction history, and a route to contest discrepancies. The proposed process should be assessed against item inspection, including intentionally incomplete evidence, before any accuracy promise is made.

#### Explanations should support correction as well as acceptance

A useful comparison identifies the attribute, points to its evidence, expresses limitations, and lets the buyer reject the inference. Explanation research creates a necessary tension: CLF10 shows that presentation can influence recommendation acceptance, while CLF11 shows that compelling explanations can leave accuracy unchanged or encourage reliance on errors. These findings are not contradictory because acceptance and correctness are different outcomes.

For Familiar, a successful explanation may lead to *not buying*. “Similar drape, but less demonstrated stretch; stretch recovery has not been established” may expose a reason to decline. Ranking should therefore consider preference mismatch and uncertainty alongside attractive similarities. A persuasive paragraph without inspectable support is a weak validation target. Test whether users can locate the source, identify unassessed properties, and correct a wrong comparison [CLF10–CLF11].

#### Conversion alone cannot establish the business benefit

CL16 frames remote shopping as ordering followed by keeping or returning; detailed experimental results were inaccessible here. This distinction nevertheless provides a useful conceptual measurement plan. A larger order count can accompany more disappointing purchases, more seller work, or more disputes. Familiar should ultimately examine completed kept transactions, buyer satisfaction after contact with the garment, refunds, complaints, capture costs, and contribution margin. No reviewed source establishes Familiar’s return reduction, sales uplift, willingness to pay, or Indonesian market demand.

### Next steps / Risks

All studies below are **proposed and unexecuted**. They refine the product’s claims and operating decisions; they do not restrict the competition prototype to an MVP. The demonstration may show a complete future experience while clearly distinguishing authored simulations from established functionality.

**1. Reference-value experiment.** Randomize participants to conventional listings; standardized capture plus attribute descriptions; the same evidence plus an unfamiliar comparison garment; or the same evidence plus their own reference. The main contrast is own-reference versus the strong standardized-evidence baseline. Include repeated garments with counterbalanced order, and measure likes/dislikes on the reference before presenting candidates. Participants predict attribute differences and expected liking, then physically inspect candidates under consistent conditions. Primary outcomes are attribute prediction error and mismatch between expected and experienced liking. Secondary outcomes include decision time, confidence calibration, and choice reversal. Ownership and personal relevance should be separated where feasible. Determine sample size from a pilot variance estimate and a preregistered meaningful effect, not an invented validation threshold [CLF02–CLF05].

**2. Condition-evidence experiment.** Compare unguided and guided seller capture for garments independently inspected by two assessors with an adjudication procedure. Record defect-location coverage, seller correction, capture completion time, retakes, and abandonment. Buyer tasks measure detection of visible flaws, false alarms, severity comprehension, and knowledge of missing evidence. Include lighting variation, occlusion, repairs, pilling, fading, and clean-looking incomplete captures. Keep photograph-visible condition separate from hygiene and smell. The prototype can simulate these cases; model sensitivity and false-negative rates require a real held-out dataset and garment-level separation [CLF07–CLF09].

**3. Explanation and badge experiment.** Hold ranking and underlying evidence constant while comparing generic scores, attribute explanations, and evidence-linked explanations with uncertainty. Include correct, incorrect, and insufficient-evidence examples. Measure acceptance of correct suggestions, rejection of wrong suggestions, evidence retrieval success, and mistaken belief that the badge certifies unassessed properties. Confidence and trust are secondary measures. Obtain consent for simulated error cases and debrief participants. Improvement requires better discrimination between reliable and unreliable advice, not simply a higher trust score [CLF10–CLF11].

**4. Marketplace and commercial follow-through.** In exploratory sessions, recruit Indonesian buyers with varied resale experience, budgets, phone capability, and garment needs; include people who avoid secondhand clothing. Observe ordinary search before offering the reference feature. Record voluntary uptake, reasons for skipping, capture burden, and reference reuse. Later randomized rollout should report kept purchases per exposed buyer, condition-related disputes, sensory/preference mismatch, reason-coded returns, repeat usage, seller time, and net contribution after processing and refunds. Analyse confidence intervals and segment interactions specified in advance. One-off demonstration usability cannot validate these commercial outcomes [CLF06–CLF11].

The central research gap is narrow and valuable: whether personal references improve calibrated expectations beyond good shared evidence at a cost buyers and sellers will accept. Existing literature supports examining that mechanism. It leaves the product-specific benefit, operational reliability, and realized business impact open.

Retained all findings, but confined material labels to visual recognition tasks. Scientific estimates of intrinsic mechanics remain conditional on measurement and capture assumptions. A classifier identifying “fabric” or a texture described as “woven” supplies neither chemical composition nor sensory equivalence. The ledger deliberately preserves the mixed sensory findings rather than asserting that video universally closes the touch gap.

### Conclusions

#### 1. Feasible outputs begin with observations

Familiar can credibly aim to organize visible evidence, recognize broad garment attributes, and compare captured behavior. Whether its models do so reliably is an unanswered empirical question. The product should store four distinct statuses: observed evidence, model estimate, seller declaration, and unknown. “Extends more in these two clips” is an observational comparison; “has greater elasticity” requires comparable force, direction, geometry, and repeated recovery measurements. [TVF1–TVF7]

| Property | Camera-supported description | Required evidence | Unresolved or unavailable conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Garment category, visible construction | Blouse-like silhouette; ribbed surface; visible lining | Full views, localized close-ups | Exact fiber blend, hidden construction |
| Surface appearance | More lustrous at the captured angles; visible fuzz or coarse pattern | Stable illumination, angle sweep, focus and scale | Skin softness, friction, itchiness |
| Drape and movement | Falls in broader folds; settles differently after the recorded motion | Comparable support and task; stable camera | Universal stiffness independent of geometry and force |
| Extension and recovery | Marked region lengthens and returns during the task | Known reference length, direction, timing | Tensile modulus, durability or recovery under unknown load |
| Show-through | Background is more visible under this setup | Matched background, light and layer count | Breathability, air permeability or thermal comfort |
| Condition | Candidate stain, pilling, hole or seam irregularity at a location | Original crop/video and coverage record | Odor, hygiene, microscopic damage, defect-free garment |
| Care label | OCR transcription, corrected by seller | Complete focused label photograph | Independent composition or brand authentication |

These boundaries are measurement logic and proposed product safeguards, not new results from the cited studies. Whole-garment drape includes cut, seams, lining, weight distribution, size, support, and motion. Personal-reference ranking must either control these variables or say explicitly that it compares garment behavior, not isolated fabric properties.

#### 2. Fashion and material datasets are foundations with mismatched targets

DeepFashion shows how clothing attributes and landmarks can structure recognition across photographic scenarios. DeepFashion2’s masks and nuisance annotations are particularly useful for identifying the garment and recording visibility before extraction. Neither dataset should be relabeled as a ground-truth database of secondhand condition or tactile satisfaction. DTD provides a model for understandable visual descriptors; its surface-oriented images are unlike many full listings. MINC addresses broad materials amid varied context, rather than distinguishing indistinguishable fiber blends. [TVF1–TVF3]

A Familiar pipeline could use these resources for initialization, then learn and evaluate on consented seller-style captures. Direct reuse requires a separate license and permitted-use check. “Pretrained” describes model history, not deployment fitness. Promptable segmentation such as SAM can help annotate garment regions, but a correctly outlined sleeve may still contain an undetected hole; transparent lace, dark clothing, overlapping hands, and folds need explicit validation. [TVF2, TVF9; application inference]

The practical domain shift includes phone sharpening, denoising, white balance, HDR, compressed messaging uploads, macro distance, mixed household light, handheld camera motion, diverse prints, and wear. These are proposed stress-test factors, not effects quantified by the fashion benchmarks. DeepFashion2's collection also involved cleaning images with large occlusions, small scales, and low resolutions; its difficulty annotations do not remove that selection boundary. Prevent background and branding shortcuts by evaluating crops, changed backgrounds, and unfamiliar sellers. A high score on curated images cannot establish performance on this target distribution. [TVF1–TVF3, TVF10]

#### 3. Motion contains information, but inverse mechanics is conditional

Bouman’s paper is a genuine physical-estimation precedent. Its samples were cut to similar dimensions, steamed, suspended from a bar, and filmed under fan-generated wind. Ground truth came from textile measurements. The modeling subset excluded fabrics lacking texture or producing specularities because optical flow became inaccurate. These details substantially narrow transfer to complete, worn garments and short phone tasks. The paper’s use of “density” also refers to area weight in the analyzed predictions; Familiar should avoid conflating this with volumetric density. [TVF4]

Bi’s temporal study supports analyzing sequences rather than treating video as an unordered collection of stills. Its perceptual target was derived from cloth animations; a model that predicts perceived stiffness has not thereby measured tactile softness. Rodriguez-Pardo strengthens the case for controlled shape information and perceptual ranking, but uses depth images in specified arrangements and fabric density as input. It cannot be cited as proof that ordinary RGB photographs alone recover equivalent mechanical parameters. [TVF5, TVF7]

Most decisively, Wijntjes’s jeans experiments produced mixed evidence: movies improved one visual–haptic matching task, while the benefit disappeared under another similarity task. This directly cautions against equating visual similarity, haptic similarity, and preference. Familiar’s central personal-reference hypothesis remains plausible and testable; it is not established by these papers. Sensory testing with actual garments is indispensable before claiming that recommendations feel closer to what buyers own. [TVF6]

#### 4. Matching should preserve differences and missingness

Fashion IQ offers a precedent for combining a reference image with comparative language, such as a changed visual attribute. Familiar can adopt the interaction principle while replacing an opaque universal resemblance score with property-specific comparisons. The proposed ranking separates category/size/style relevance, observed behavior similarity, evidence adequacy, and buyer preferences. A normal keyword or natural-language search must remain available without a wardrobe upload. [TVF8; product requirement]

Missing extension evidence should reduce eligibility for an extension-sensitive comparison; it must not be imputed as “average stretch.” Similarity can be calculated only over compatible descriptors with disclosed coverage, while a minimum-evidence rule prevents sparsely documented items receiving artificially high ranks. A buyer who prioritizes drape may accept a less similar sheen. Condition belongs beside matching and must not be averaged away by attractive appearance. These are proposed ranking contracts, to be compared against simpler visual retrieval and ordinary-filter baselines. [TVF2–TVF8; application inference]

#### 5. Evidence links are stronger than persuasive explanations

Guo’s calibration work motivates checking probability quality rather than presenting raw model scores as confidence. Ovadia shows why a calibration fitted on familiar data cannot be assumed reliable after distribution shift. Geifman’s selective-classification framework supports “cannot assess,” recapture, or human review, with risk measured among accepted predictions and coverage reported separately. These methods do not create a guarantee for an untested marketplace. [TVF10–TVF11]

For condition flags, show the original frame or crop, location, capture date, and seller response. “Possible hole at left cuff; inspect this crop” is more accountable than an unexplained quality number. Grad-CAM can assist model debugging; it is not proof of a defect or a complete causal explanation. Adebayo’s sanity checks justify testing explanations against model/data randomization before relying on them. Generated text should be constrained to stored evidence records, and each statement should have a valid media pointer. [TVF12]

#### 6. Gaussian splatting and Three.js belong to presentation

Gaussian splatting can provide novel viewing angles from multi-view capture; it does not automatically infer fiber chemistry, mechanics, odor, or touch. Its original objective is radiance-field rendering. Reconstructing moving cloth or specular, transparent, fine surfaces introduces additional capture and modeling difficulties; standard static reconstruction should not be assumed to preserve every small defect. Original photos and task videos remain the auditable evidence. [TVF13; application inference]

[Three.js](https://threejs.org/) is a JavaScript 3D library. Familiar could use it to display a reconstruction, illustrative garment animation, or linked evidence hotspot. The renderer is separate from the inference model. A beautiful simulated drape in the competition prototype can explain an intended interaction, but should be labeled illustrative unless generated and validated from that garment’s measured profile. Gaussian splatting is optional; neither it nor Three.js is necessary for a useful side-by-side evidence comparison.

### Next steps / risks

#### Proposed capture and annotation protocol

The following protocol is a research design, not an executed study. Capture front/back full views, label, seams and high-wear regions; a hanging view; a standardized gentle movement; a marked, safe pull/release if suitable; and an optional controlled show-through view. Ask the seller to confirm the exact listing garment, disclose alterations, and correct OCR. Retain originals, task/version metadata, masks, time intervals, and crop coordinates. Separate user footage from any enhanced or reconstructed display. [TVF2–TVF7, TVF12]

Use a stable phone, fixed distance with scale marker, diffuse light, neutral background, and consistent hanging support. Match clip task and local region across reference and candidate. Flag blur, clipping, occlusion, excessive camera movement, and incomplete coverage. Do not direct forceful stretching of fragile garments. Where force is not measured, describe recorded extension rather than force-normalized elasticity. Quantify whether each control improves repeatability enough to justify seller effort. [TVF4, TVF7; proposed adaptation]

Build annotation rules before training. Record defects by type, location, visibility, extent, and ambiguity; include shadows, prints, decorative distressing, loose lint, and intentional seam features as hard negatives. Independent annotators should label originals before seeing model output, with textile-informed adjudication. Record disagreements rather than silently forcing certainty. Tag unseen or occluded regions as “not observed”; absence of a flag on an unseen region is not a negative example. Record measurement directions for anisotropic fabrics. [TVF2–TVF7; proposed adaptation]

#### Evaluation matrix and acceptance logic

| Test | Reference and metric | Critical split/control |
|---|---|---|
| Capture usability | Human task-completion labels; acceptance error, recapture frequency, completion time | New sellers and devices; accessibility and small-space conditions |
| Segmentation and tracking | Expert masks/trajectories; IoU, boundary error, temporal drift | Lace, dark garments, hands, changing background |
| Condition detection | Localized expert flags; class precision/recall, precision–recall curves, localization agreement | Rare defects, hard negatives, small defects, unseen garments |
| Descriptor repeatability | Same garment across repeated operators/setups; within-garment variability and rank stability | Phone, light, framing, support and motion changes |
| Physical-property estimates, if pursued | Independent measured properties; MAE and rank correlation by property | Measured force, region geometry and direction; held-out fabrics |
| Reference matching | Blinded property judgments; pairwise agreement, Recall@k and nDCG with stated relevance rules | Same style/different behavior and different style/similar behavior |
| Confidence and abstention | Reliability plots, calibration error, Brier/NLL; risk–coverage curves | Independent calibration set and shifted test sets |
| Evidence explanations | Media-pointer validity, statement support rate, user localization/comprehension | Deliberate mismatches and unsupported-claim challenges |
| Buyer expectation accuracy | Predictions before touching versus blinded physical evaluation | Ordinary listing, guided video, AI profile and personal-reference conditions |

Report confidence intervals and denominators; choose sample sizes using desired precision and realistic defect prevalence, rather than an invented round target. Split by garment identity and preferably seller/capture session: frames or repeat clips of the same item must not straddle training and testing. Maintain a final untouched prospective seller test set. Stratify results by category, defect class, lighting, device and evidence completeness. Calibration and abstention thresholds are fitted without the final test labels. [TVF2, TVF4–TVF7, TVF10–TVF11; protocol proposal]

For sensory validation, separately measure predicted drape/stretch/appearance, actual tactile judgments, and personal preference. Randomize presentation order; blind participants to price, brand and model claims where possible. Compare personal-reference ranking with ordinary search, image-only retrieval, and guided video without AI. Test whether the additional layer improves expectation accuracy beyond capture quality alone. Satisfaction, return reduction and reuse require later real transactions and longitudinal observation; laboratory agreement cannot prove those business outcomes. [TVF6–TVF8; proposed study]

#### Restaging, fraud and seller confirmation

Threat-model ordinary presentation manipulation as well as technical tampering: stronger pulls to imply stretch; vigorous shaking to imply fluidity; light changes to conceal stains; folded sleeves hiding damage; stock-media substitution; washed or steamed capture followed by changed condition; and a different shipped item. These are prospective threat scenarios, not measured attack rates. Session prompts, original-media retention, repeat captures, consistency checks and dispute review can raise evidential quality, but cannot ensure inventory identity or truthful disclosure. Seller confirmation is accountability, not independent verification.

Keep “AI flag,” “seller confirmed,” “seller disputed,” and “human reviewed” separate. A captured-media badge should state exactly what was checked and which regions remain unseen. Known staining or pilling must persist in the listing after confirmation; removing a model suggestion requires an auditable correction. Do not describe the outcome as guaranteed quality, authenticity, cleanliness, or defect-free certification.

**Research acceptance gate.** Familiar’s claims become defensible only when each published descriptor has a defined capture protocol, target label, held-out evaluation, uncertainty policy, and original evidence link. The present literature supports the components and the research direction [TVF1–TVF13]; it leaves end-to-end phone performance, tactile-reference usefulness, seller effort, and robustness to restaging unanswered. The competition prototype can demonstrate the intended complete experience while clearly distinguishing simulated examples from evaluated capabilities.

### Conclusions

#### 1. The product judges should experience

Familiar is a clothing marketplace with three cooperating layers: **search normally, match personally, and inspect better evidence**. Home displays desirable garments, recognizable categories, useful search, prices, sizes, and seller information. A person looking for “a black blouse under Rp150,000” can shop immediately. Familiar Match becomes an optional enhancement: choose a favorite garment already in a private wardrobe, or add one, and compare relevant visible behavior. Familiar Evidence makes condition inspectable through source photos, short clips, marked regions, capture coverage, seller confirmation, and missing-information labels.

The demonstrator should feel operational from discovery to receipt and from seller capture to publication. A listing has one coherent identity everywhere. Saving it updates Favorites; buying it changes its availability; a seller correction changes its condition profile; deleting a reference removes it from the wardrobe and future comparison choices. Judges can diverge from the narrated route and still find intentional screens. Simulations should preserve these consequences.

Use a restrained, editorial visual language: warm off-white background, dark readable text, one deep green or indigo accent, generous garment imagery, clear typography, and understated motion. Show material detail without turning the marketplace into a scientific instrument panel. Product pages lead with garment, price, size, seller, delivery, and condition. Evidence and explanations provide depth one action away. English competition copy can coexist with an Indonesian locale fixture; currency and size conventions stay internally consistent.

A short presentation introduction should say: “This is a vision demonstrator using prepared garments and simulated services.” In the app, identify illustrative 3D assets and simulated checkout where these affect a decision. Do not sprinkle implementation explanations across ordinary shopping screens. Neither ratings nor transaction counts should imply real adoption: use explicit example identities and omit invented popularity claims.

Condition, availability, publication, and moderation are separate state dimensions. A fully photographed garment may have visible damage; a seller-confirmed note may remain disputed; a published listing may be sold. “Complete evidence” must never become shorthand for “good condition.”

#### 3. Recommended demonstrator architecture

Choose a React/TypeScript web application, Vite build, URL-based routing, typed domain models, and a small deterministic fixture service. React and Vite's documented support makes this a straightforward choice [AF1]. A static Figma prototype is a useful design checkpoint but struggles to preserve order, correction, and deletion consequences. A live cloud backend introduces unnecessary demo failure modes. The recommended application represents the complete experience while keeping those services simulated.

Separate four modules: presentation components; domain transitions; service interface; prepared assets and fixtures. Components request operations such as searchListings, compareGarments, reviewFinding, publishListing, reserveListing and submitOrder. A fixture adapter returns typed promises using predictable short delays. A later network adapter could use the same contracts, but replacing an adapter would not by itself make the production system complete.

Use explicit state transitions rather than many unrelated booleans. For example, a capture moves through draft → capturing → quality review → analysis → seller review → ready, with recapture and failed branches. An order moves through reservation → payment pending → confirmed → shipment → delivered, with expiry and dispute branches. Prevent double confirmation and impossible combinations.

Persist demo state in IndexedDB, with in-memory fallback if storage is denied [AF3]. Store asset identifiers rather than repeatedly duplicating media blobs. Fixtures use a schema version, fixed logical clock, stable identifiers, and a seed. A presenter-only panel selects scenario, slow network, camera denial, failed analysis, payment decline or moderation hold, and resets all mutations. Keep this panel outside normal navigation. Judge actions should work without the panel.

Prepare a modest but credible example catalog, proposed at 30–50 garments across blouses, knitwear, dresses and additional browse categories. Give the main three comparison candidates rich evidence, and other garments intentional partial profiles. Include both attractive matches and an aesthetically similar but less comparable candidate. Use two example private references and seller drafts. All fixture observations must be authored from the prepared footage or clearly designated scenario data, never mislabeled as automated discoveries.

Use HTML video and photographs for the evidence pathway. An optional Three.js/react-three-fiber viewer can provide a rotatable authored GLB, garment silhouette exploration, or an attractive overview [AF2]. Load it only when opened. Label it “Illustrative 3D preview”; keep its origin in the media manifest. It does not certify the listing, reproduce hand feel, or prove its fabric mechanics. Real evidence remains the original prepared photos and footage. Animated drape in an authored asset must not be used to justify a condition or comparison statement. An equivalent image carousel must carry the whole purchasing flow when WebGL is unavailable.

#### 6. Proposed production stack and boundaries

Production should begin with a modular backend rather than prematurely separated microservices. Recommended components are a React web client; FastAPI/Python API with OpenAPI contracts; managed PostgreSQL for transactional records and pgvector for candidate retrieval; private object storage and a CDN for approved derivatives; a durable job queue; isolated media/vision workers; and a separate operator console [AF6–AF8]. Use managed identity and a region-appropriate licensed payment provider selected after availability, settlement, seller onboarding and regulatory requirements are checked. The demonstrator must not depend on a live provider.

Python supports integration with computer-vision research without making inference an API request's blocking workload. Database transactions govern reservations and orders; workers handle media asynchronously. Queue delivery may repeat, so handlers require idempotency, bounded retries, dead-letter review and observable job status. A transactional outbox avoids announcing publication before the corresponding database change commits. These are proposed engineering controls.

The web client can share much of the demonstrator's presentation code. Search-engine indexing, rendering strategy and native-app needs require a later production decision. If reliable on-device OCR or capture guidance justifies native clients, ML Kit is one verified possible component [AF9]. Browser and native capture capabilities are not interchangeable, and an OCR module alone does not classify condition or fabric.

#### 7. Data model and contracts

Keep normalized transaction data separate from immutable evidence/profile revisions. Use opaque IDs, owner IDs, timestamps, schema versions and explicit access scope.

| Entity | Essential fields and constraints |
| --- | --- |
| Account / seller | Account ID, roles, seller identity status, locale, consent records; sensitive verification data separated. |
| Listing | Seller ID, title, category/construction, size, dimensions and units, price/currency, declarations, revision, availability, publication and moderation state. |
| Capture session | Owner, purpose listing/reference, protocol version, tasks, device metadata, quality checks, completion and consent scope. |
| Media asset | Owner, private/public scope, object key, hash, media type, original/derivative relationship, rights/provenance, duration, retention state. |
| Garment profile | Listing/reference ID, profile revision, protocol/model versions, properties, compatible capture domains and evidence coverage. |
| Property observation | Descriptor, value/units when valid, support level, unknown reason, evidence references, model estimate or seller declaration. |
| Condition finding | Type, garment region, source frame/timestamps, suggestion, seller response, review status and supersession link. |
| Wardrobe reference | Private owner, name, selected profile, retention preference; excluded from public catalog. |
| Comparison | Reference/candidate profile revisions, active priorities, comparable feature set, differences, abstentions and evidence pointers. |
| Reservation / order | Item revision, buyer, expiry, price snapshot, payment status, fulfillment and dispute state; single active ownership transfer. |
| Feedback / audit | Subject, response, consent; actor, event, reason and version, with minimization and access restrictions. |

Every evidence pointer identifies an asset, time range or frame, and normalized region coordinates. Hashes support integrity checks after ingestion; they do not prove garment ownership, recording date, completeness or truthfulness.

API contracts should specify authorization, request schema, success/error schema, concurrency behavior and idempotency. Proposed endpoints include GET /listings with filters and cursors; GET /listings/{id}/evidence with a profile revision; POST /captures returning an upload manifest; POST /captures/{id}/complete returning a 202 job identifier; GET /jobs/{id}; PATCH /drafts/{id}/findings/{findingId} with confirm/correct/unsure; POST /drafts/{id}/publish; POST /comparisons; DELETE /references/{id}; POST /reservations; and POST /orders with an idempotency key. Publication and order submission use expected revisions to reject stale edits.

Comparisons return property-specific outcomes and an overall status of comparable, partial, or insufficient evidence. Errors return code, safe message, retryable flag, request ID and relevant recovery action. Return a conflict for sold or changed inventory; an invalid-input response for missing dimensions; an asynchronous job state for processing; and a structured abstention for unavailable properties. Never turn unavailable evidence into an empty successful result.

Domain events include capture.completed, media.accepted, analysis.failed, profile.drafted, finding.reviewed, listing.published, listing.held, reservation.expired, order.confirmed and reference.deletion.requested. Envelopes contain event ID, schema version, subject ID, subject revision, occurred-at time and correlation ID. Avoid placing raw private media or full addresses on the event bus.

#### 8. Capture, media and inference pipeline

Seller guidance requests front/back and worn-area photos, care-label photo if present, surface close-up, hanging/movement clip, and optional gentle extension/release or compatible show-through task. Never ask users to damage clothing. Category-specific tasks and an accessible upload alternative reduce unnecessary friction. A buyer reference follows compatible tasks, but incomplete capture may still serve ordinary visual organization.

Production sequence: authorize session → issue constrained upload access → validate media → preserve original → generate playback derivatives → assess task quality → segment/track relevant regions → extract observations → draft profile → human review → moderation/publication → index approved revision.

Presigned URLs are access credentials; constrain scope and expiry, protect them from logs, and do not expose originals through public buckets [AF8]. Validate actual file format, byte and duration limits, decoding behavior, and malicious content rather than trusting extensions or MIME declarations [AF9]. Run decoding in isolated workers. FFmpeg can produce posters, thumbnails and bounded-resolution playback versions [AF8]. Preserve transformations, timestamps and crop relationships; generated cleanup must not erase damage in evidence.

The pipeline may combine segmentation, temporal features, OCR and condition models, selected only after category-specific evaluation. Store applied protocol, model version and quality checks. Draft observations should distinguish observable movement from mechanical measurements: extension without controlled force is not modulus; label text is a declaration, not chemical verification; surface appearance is not softness against skin. Seller confirmation adds accountability, not independent laboratory validation.

#### 9. Retrieval, reranking and grounded explanation

Ordinary search retrieves available, eligible listings through structured filters and text search. Natural language may propose filters, but the user can inspect and change them. Authorized seller feeds can expand the catalog later; “platform-wide” means Familiar's indexed authorized inventory, not access to every external marketplace.

Optional Match retrieves category-compatible candidates using appearance/behavior representations, then reranks using comparable observations and user priorities. Eligibility filters remain mandatory before final display. Exact vector search is a sensible baseline; approximate indexing needs measured recall under size, category and availability filters because post-filtered approximate results can be sparse [AF7].

For comparable features A, an illustrative distance is:

$$
D(r,s)=\frac{\sum_{i\in A} w_i q_i |z_{r,i}-z_{s,i}|}
{\sum_{i\in A} w_i q_i}.
$$

Feature scales must be normalized within compatible domains; weights express priorities; support factors require empirical justification. Missing values are omitted, not assigned zero. Require positive support and a minimum meaningful coverage policy. Separate size suitability, style relevance, condition and behavior rather than hiding them in a universal “quality” score. This formula is a design illustration, not a validated algorithm.

Show confidence as explainable support labels such as sufficient, limited and unavailable, with reasons. Do not display probabilistic accuracy or “92% same feel” without calibration. “Similar movement in these clips; less observed extension” is preferable to “feels identical.” Evidence coverage is not confidence, and model certainty is not truth.

Use deterministic templates for standard comparisons. If a language model answers questions, supply only authorized structured observations and their evidence references; require a schema and reject unsupported output. Treat seller text, OCR and uploads as untrusted data, never instructions [AF9]. The assistant must not invent citations, execute seller requests or approve findings. For odor, hygiene, skin feel, authenticity or unavailable breathability evidence, answer that the property cannot be established and offer an appropriate seller question. Preserve the underlying unknown even if the seller supplies an unverified answer.

#### 10. Privacy, security and operational trust

Owned wardrobe references are private by default, excluded from public search, and processed only for the owner's authorized comparisons. Ask for camera permission at the point of capture, provide upload alternatives, and stop camera tracks on exit [AF3]. Avoid collecting faces, voices, room details or precise location where unnecessary; strip irrelevant public metadata while retaining essential processing provenance securely.

Use role-based authorization and ownership checks at every API and object boundary. PostgreSQL row security is defense in depth; privileged service roles and table owners can bypass policies, so protect and audit them [AF6]. Separate seller editing, buyer access and operator review permissions. Keep payment credentials with the provider. Rate-limit uploads and expensive inference; encrypt traffic and stored private data; use secret management, short-lived access and audited administrative actions.

Proposed retention defaults: delete abandoned capture drafts after 30 days; retain a private reference until deletion or a clearly stated inactivity policy; retain live listing evidence plus a defined dispute period; retain transaction records according to the applicable legal schedule. Deletion removes access promptly and schedules derivatives, embeddings and replicas for purge; disclose bounded backup expiry and any required legal hold. These values are proposed operational choices requiring jurisdiction-specific review, not statements of law. Training use requires separate opt-in consent and withdrawal handling.

Moderation checks prohibited listings, abusive media, misleading condition claims and suspected manipulation. A disputed finding triggers visible review rather than silent removal from history. Sellers can correct mistakes and appeal a hold. Buyers can report evidence inconsistency and submit dispute media. Human operators decide unresolved cases; the camera cannot certify every hidden defect or transaction outcome.

### 1. The strongest coherent story

**Familiar is a secondhand clothing marketplace that combines ordinary shopping, clearer condition evidence, and optional discovery using a garment the buyer already knows.** Its organizing problem is an information gap: the buyer cannot inspect the item physically, while sellers struggle to communicate its current condition and material behavior consistently. The condition and familiarity mechanisms address two questions within the same purchase decision: “What am I actually buying?” and “How does it compare with something I know?”

Standard search is the accessible entrance. Guided seller capture creates a reusable evidence asset. Condition assistance organizes observable wear; optional Familiar Match compares supported appearance and movement descriptors with a private reference. Both outputs link original evidence. Similarity and condition can differ; keep these judgments separate.

Recommended public line: **“Search normally. Match personally. Inspect the evidence.”** A longer pitch sentence is: “Familiar helps people choose secondhand clothing through structured condition evidence and optional comparisons with clothes they already love wearing.” “Feel familiar” can remain evocative brand copy, but the adjacent explanation must make clear that the camera compares visible behavior, rather than transmitting touch.

The social argument should name affected people before mentioning sustainability: buyers with limited budgets who cannot afford disappointing purchases, and local resale sellers who need credible listings without laboratory equipment. These are proposed priority segments, not established research findings. Actual first-hand accounts must be supplied by team members or documented interviews. The conversation does not establish interviews, measured frustration rates, saved money, seller demand, or improved purchase outcomes. ## 9. Financial reconciliation, commercial experiments and measurable milestones

### 9.1 One active financial model

The principal scenario is marketplace commission: no ordinary buyer fee and no per-listing charge are assumed in the base contribution table. The seller pays a hypothetical 10% on merchandise value; buyer-funded shipping passes through. Optional future subscription/API revenue is absent from the model. This avoids accidentally counting a software-service market and a marketplace take rate against the same transactions.

All scenario prices, commission rates, costs and volumes are hypotheses. Exact arithmetic does not make inputs empirically true. C01 calculations were recomputed by the coordinator; they agree with the market chapter. The source of the price assumptions is the proposed model, not a payment provider or transaction dataset.

### 9.2 Annual volume stress-test

This table holds the **base** unit economics constant: IDR 200,000 merchandise/order, 10% commission, IDR 18,000 variable cost/order, and IDR 30m fixed cash costs/month. Fixed costs exclude processing, review/support and infrastructure already allocated in per-order variable cost; payroll and overhead must be assigned once. The table deliberately does not mix the earlier reach scenarios' different garment prices with this common-price test. Acquisition, taxes, capital expenditure and working-capital effects are still excluded.

| Completed annual orders | GMV | Commission revenue | Variable cost | Contribution | Fixed cash costs | Result before acquisition/tax |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10,000 | IDR 2,000m | IDR 200m | IDR 180m | IDR 20m | IDR 360m | IDR -340m |
| 30,000 | IDR 6,000m | IDR 600m | IDR 540m | IDR 60m | IDR 360m | IDR -300m |
| 80,000 | IDR 16,000m | IDR 1,600m | IDR 1,440m | IDR 160m | IDR 360m | IDR -200m |
| 180,000 | IDR 36,000m | IDR 3,600m | IDR 3,240m | IDR 360m | IDR 360m | IDR 0m |


Under these inputs, the platform needs 180,000 completed annual orders to cover IDR 360m annual fixed costs **before acquisition and taxes**. A central reach scenario of 30,000 orders/year is six times smaller. It is not credible to call that scenario profitable merely because its GMV is IDR 6bn.

The upside contribution of IDR 12,550/order uses the chapter 3 upside parameters: IDR 250,000 merchandise price and 12% commission, distinct from the common-price base table. At that contribution, 30,000 orders yield IDR 376.5m and IDR 16.5m after the same fixed costs, before acquisition/tax. If 10,000 newly acquired buyers each cost the hypothetical IDR 12,000 to acquire, that subtracts IDR 120m and produces a deficit of IDR 103.5m. This CAC excludes the recurring IDR 2,000 per-order promotional subsidy already in contribution; returning buyers are not reacquired automatically. Stronger unit economics help; they still require acquisition and retention proof.

### 9.3 What would improve the model

| Lever | Plausible mechanism | Cost or trade-off to measure |
|---|---|---|
| Better capture completion and sell-through | Spread capture expense across more completed sales. | Assisted onboarding and support may raise costs. |
| More useful repeat purchases | Recover acquisition over repeated contribution. | Discounts and engagement spending can consume the gain. |
| Efficient human review | Reduce per-order support burden without hiding problems. | False flags, missed issues and unfair appeals must be monitored. |
| Bundled purchases | Spread shipping/payment costs over several garments. | Multi-seller fulfilment, partial returns and inventory coordination become harder. |
| Merchant workflow subscription | Charge for batch tools, exports or operations utility. | Prove standalone merchant benefit; do not sell a favourable evidence finding. |
| Category/price concentration | Avoid low-value items with disproportionate fixed handling costs. | Limits affordability/reach and may exclude parts of the mission. |
| Lower loss and discrepancy rates | Reduce refunds, disputes and unrecovered settlements. | Achieved only through observed outcomes, not a assumed AI accuracy number. |

Each lever is an experiment. Raising commission by one percentage point adds IDR 2,000 at the base price, but seller exit or lower liquidity could offset it. Reducing capture cost by generating synthetic evidence would violate the trust promise. Subsidies should have a stated cap and purpose, and remain visible in contribution.
